How this map works

An earthquake happens when giant slabs of rock deep underground suddenly slip and shake. Aotearoa New Zealand sits right where two of these slabs push against each other, so we feel a lot of earthquakes!

  • The dots are shake detectors. Scientists have placed hundreds of machines called seismometers all around the country — each dot is one of them. When shaking reaches a dot it grows bigger. The bigger the dot, the harder the ground is moving.

  • The rings are the earthquake's waves. A quake sends out waves, a bit like ripples when you drop a stone in a pond. The speedy P wave (dashed ring) arrives first, then the slower but stronger S wave (solid ring). They spread out from the epicentre — the spot right above where the quake began.

  • The wiggly line is real. The line along the bottom is a seismogram — an actual recording of the ground jiggling up and down. Drag along it to travel backwards and forwards through the earthquake.

✦ Hover over any dot to see that station's name and its very own wiggly recording.
